# 🏥 TNHD Official EMS Guidelines

Official operational and behavioral guidelines for the **Thalainagaram Health Department (TNHD)**.

---

## 1. ✅ What We Should Do (Code of Conduct)

* **Be Respectful:** Always stay calm, friendly, and professional when talking to players.
* **Maintain On-Duty Conduct:** Personnel are expected to maintain character integrity throughout their shift. Disruptive behavior, unattended duty status, unrealistic actions, or misuse of external information will not be tolerated.
* **Follow City Rules:** EMS follows city rules plus our internal TNHD EMS guidelines.
* **Listen to Seniors:** If a higher-rank EMS gives instructions, follow them — they are there to help you learn and grow.

---

## 2. ❌ What We Should NOT Do

* **No Trolling or Conflict:** Do not troll, provoke, or intentionally disrupt anyone. Do not argue or create conflict during active scenes.
* **Maintain Character Integrity:** Maintain character integrity at all times. Do not use external or out-of-character (OOC) information.
* **No Forced RP:** Do not force actions or outcomes on other individuals.
* **No Misuse of EMS Authority:** Do not misuse EMS authority, equipment, or privileges.
* **Medical Realism:** Act in a medically realistic manner at all times.
* **Strict Weapon Ban:** Carrying weapons while on duty is strictly prohibited.
* **No Custody Bypassing:** Do not use EMS status to bypass arrest, death, or police custody procedures.

---

## 3. ⏱️ Staying Ready on Duty (Duty Guidelines)

* **Clock In / Clock Out:** Clock in when you start working. Clock out when you are done or taking a long break.
* **Uniform & Gear:** Wear your official EMS uniform at all times while working. Use EMS vehicles only when on duty.
* **Driving Rules:** Drive safely at all times. Use lights and sirens only for real emergencies. No reckless driving unless it fits an emergency response.
* **Scene Safety:** Do not enter a crime scene until police say it is safe. Make sure you and others are safe before helping a patient.

---

## 4. 🪜 Rank Hierarchy — "From Rookie to Chief"

### 👑 Leadership (Level 4)
* **Director of Medical Service:** Heads the entire medical department.
* **Dean / Assistant Dean:** Helps manage training, standards, and department operations.
* **Chief Medical Superintendent:** Oversees daily hospital and field EMS functioning.

### 🔬 Senior Doctors (Level 3)
* **Senior Surgeon:** Expert surgeon managing major surgical procedures.
* **Junior Surgeon:** Assists in surgeries and advanced field trauma treatments.

### 🩺 Experienced Doctors (Level 2)
* **Senior Consultant:** Handles major medical cases and guides junior personnel.
* **Junior Consultant:** Supports diagnostics and treatment planning.
* **Senior Physician:** Experienced general medical doctor.
* **Junior Physician:** Handles basic patient care under supervision.

### 🧑‍⚕️ Junior Doctors (Level 1)
* **Senior Resident:** Advanced trainee managing field and hospital cases.
* **Junior Resident:** Early-stage trainee learning core medical duties.
* **Intern:** New medical trainee in clinical orientation.

### 🚑 Trainees (Level 0)
* **Paramedic:** EMS first responder handling primary emergency calls in the field.

---

## 5. 🏥 How We Treat Our Patients

### Realistic Medical Practice
* Always assess the patient before taking any treatment action.
* Perform basic medical procedures such as CPR, bandaging, and injury stabilization when appropriate.
* Use simple medical terminology when possible — accuracy matters, but perfection is not required.

### Scene Flow & Professional Conduct
* Follow the natural flow of the situation already in progress.
* Do not rush medical care or force outcomes on others.
* Allow scenes to develop logically and respectfully.

### Patient Transport Procedures
* Patients with serious injuries must be transported to the hospital.
* Act out the full process: ambulance arrival, patient loading, transport, and handover at the hospital ER.
* Treat transport as a core part of the medical response, not a shortcut.

### Revival Protocol
> ⚠️ **Mandatory Rule:** Revivals are permitted **only after visible medical effort**. Perform appropriate steps such as CPR and injury stabilization before attempting a revival. Instant revives or unrealistic actions are strictly forbidden. Every revival must show clear medical involvement first.

## 6. 📈 How You Rank Up (Promotion Criteria)

Promotions are awarded based on how you perform and grow within TNHD. Leadership evaluates:

1. **Attitude:** Maintaining a calm, respectful, and professional approach in all situations.
2. **Discipline:** Following department procedures, protocols, and command structure at all times.
3. **Attendance:** Showing up on time, remaining active on duty, and being available when needed.
4. **Cases Seen:** Actively responding to and handling medical situations with proper care and involvement.
5. **Certification:** Completing required training and holding valid department-approved qualifications.

---

## 7. 🚨 "When the Siren Sings!" — Siren Etiquette

* **Use Sirens for Real Emergencies Only:** Turn on lights and sirens only when responding to urgent/life-threatening calls.
* **Non-Urgent Driving:** If a call is not urgent, drive with just lights or no emergency signals.
* **Intersection Precaution:** Slow down at intersections — even with sirens on. Safety first!
* **Arrival Protocol:** Turn off sirens as soon as you reach the scene or the hospital.
* **No Siren Spamming:** Never spam sirens for fun — it is disruptive and creates confusion.
* **Core Rule:** *Safety over speed! When the siren sings, it means someone needs help — make it count!*

## 9. 🚑 "From Call to Care" — EMS Quick Procedure

Follow this 7-step field response protocol for every dispatch:

1. **Wait for Assignment:** Do not respond until a senior doctor or dispatcher assigns the case to you.
2. **Confirm Response on Radio:** Broadcast: `"EMS-XX attending the assigned call."`
3. **On Arrival:** Broadcast: `"EMS-XX arrived on scene."` Check surroundings for safety. If an active shooting is happening $\rightarrow$ stage and wait for police. If safe $\rightarrow$ begin diagnosis.
4. **Radio the Diagnosis:** Example broadcast: `"EMS-XX: Patient has GSW to leg, bleeding moderate."`
5. **Transport Patient:** Load patient onto stretcher/ambulance safely and drive (Code 1/2/3 based on condition).
6. **Provide Hospital Treatment:** Treat wounds, stabilize, and complete medical care at hospital ER.
7. **Radio Completion:** Broadcast: `"EMS-XX: Treatment completed, returning to service."`

## 10. 🔐 Keep Our Gear Safe!

* **Off-Duty Return:** Return all EMS equipment when you go off-duty — no disappearing tools!
* **Pre-Shift Inventory Check:** Always check that you have sufficient medical tools before heading to any scene.
* **Equipment Responsibility:** You are responsible for any equipment issued to you. Handle it with care.
* **No Storage in Personal Cars:** Do not store medical items in personal vehicles or ambulances — keep them in official EMS storage lockers only.
* **Gear Respect:** Treat EMS gear like gold — without it, we cannot save lives!
* 🔔 **Note:** *Sharing is caring — but not when it comes to EMS equipment!*
