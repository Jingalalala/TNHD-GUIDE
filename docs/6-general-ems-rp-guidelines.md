# 🚑 General EMS Roleplay Guidelines

Welcome to the **General EMS Roleplay Guidelines** for the Thalainagarm Health Department (TNHD). In addition to mastering the TNHD Tablet and operational procedures, every doctor must maintain high-quality roleplay (RP) standards at all times.

---

## 1. 🎭 Core EMS RP Principles

### 1. Patient Care Over Everything
Medical roleplay is the heart of EMS RP. Never rush a treatment or revive a player instantly without performing appropriate `/me` and `/do` roleplay actions.

### 2. Strict EMS Neutrality (Equal Treatment Policy)
As a healthcare provider, you are legally and morally neutral.
* You must treat **all citizens equally**, regardless of whether they are police officers, gang members, criminals, or civilians.
* Never deny medical care to a patient based on their in-game alignment, faction, or past behavior.
* Do not call police on a criminal patient unless legally required by server law after medical stabilization is complete.

### 3. Fear RP & Value of Life
EMS personnel are not superheroes or combatants.
* **Your life is your priority:** If you are threatened with a weapon or caught in a firefight, comply with demands and value your life.
* Never enter an active crime scene or gunfight to revive someone while bullets are still flying.

### 4. No Corrupt EMS Roleplay
Unless explicitly granted High Command or server-approved corrupt roleplay permission:
* EMS personnel may **never** sell, give away, or abuse medical supplies (Medkits, Bandages, Syringes, Morphine, Blood Bags).
* EMS personnel may **never** assist criminals in escaping police custody or tamper with medical reports.

## 2. 🩺 Medical Roleplay Commands (`/me` & `/do`)

Medical RP relies heavily on descriptive text commands to communicate physical actions and query patient conditions.

### Essential Commands Overview
* `/me` – Describes an **action** performed by your character (e.g., `/me kneels down and checks the patient's carotid pulse`).
* `/do` – Asks or states an **environmental fact or condition** (e.g., `/do Is the patient conscious?` or `/do Pulse is weak and rapid at 110 BPM`).

### Step-by-Step Medical Examination & Treatment

#### Step 1: Initial Assessment & Safety Check
```text
/me kneels beside the patient and puts on medical latex gloves
/do Is the patient conscious or responsive?
```
*(Wait for the patient to reply in `/do` before proceeding)*

#### Step 2: Checking Vital Signs
```text
/me shines a penlight into the patient's eyes to check pupil response
/do How do the pupils react to light?
/me places two fingers on the neck to feel for a carotid pulse
/do Is there a strong pulse present?
```

#### Step 3: Wound Inspection & Diagnosis
```text
/me carefully cuts open the patient's shirt to inspect the chest and torso
/do What visible injuries, bleeding, or trauma are present?
```

#### Step 4: Treatment Protocols by Condition

* **Gunshot Wounds (GSW):**
  ```text
  /me applies sterile gauze and pressure to stop arterial bleeding
  /me prepares a chest seal and applies it over the entrance wound
  /me starts an IV line in the left arm and administers normal saline
  ```
* **Blunt Force Trauma / Crash Injuries:**
  ```text
  /me places a cervical collar around the patient's neck to stabilize the spine
  /me secures the patient onto a spine board using straps
  ```
* **Cardiac Arrest / CPR & AED:**
  ```text
  /me places hands on the center of the chest and begins 30 chest compressions
  /me places AED pads on the patient's upper right and lower left chest
  /do Does the AED analyze a shockable rhythm?
  /me charges the AED, shouts "Clear!", and delivers a shock
  ```

## 3. 🚨 Response Codes & Scene Safety (Staging)

### Emergency Response Codes

| Response Code | Definition | Light & Siren Usage | Driving Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Code 1** | Routine / Non-Urgent | No Lights, No Sirens | Standard speed, obey all traffic laws. |
| **Code 2** | Urgent Response | Lights ON, Sirens OFF (Siren at intersections) | Moderate speed, exercise caution at red lights. |
| **Code 3** | Emergency / Life-Threatening | Lights ON, Sirens ON | High speed response, proceed carefully through intersections. |

### Scene Staging Protocol

**Staging** means stopping your ambulance at a safe distance from a dangerous scene until police/law enforcement declare the area safe.

1. **When to Stage:** Active shootings, gang wars, hostage situations, robbery in progress, or armed conflicts.
2. **Procedure:**
   * Park 1–2 blocks away behind cover.
   * Notify Dispatch via radio: `[Unit Callsign] staging at [Location] awaiting PD scene clearance.`
   * **Do NOT enter** the hot zone until PD broadcasts: `Scene is clear for EMS.`

Clear, concise radio etiquette is mandatory to maintain emergency channel availability.

### Radio Transmission Format
`[Your Unit Callsign] to [Dispatch / Recipient] – [Message]`

* **Example:** `Unit 102 to Dispatch – Arrived on scene at Pillbox Hill, assessing patient.`

## 5. 🏥 Patient Transport & Hospital Handover

### Transport Guidelines
1. **Stabilize Before Transport:** Never load a patient into an ambulance while they are actively bleeding out without basic trauma stabilization.
2. **Stretcher Usage:** Use proper stretcher mechanics (`/me deploys stretcher and secures patient`).
3. **En-Route Care:** Monitor vitals in the back of the rig during transport.

### Hospital Handover Procedure
When arriving at TNHD Hospital / ER:
1. Transfer patient from stretcher to hospital bed.
2. Brief attending doctors or log the case in the TNHD Tablet.
3. Provide summary: `Patient sustained 2 GSW to lower torso, stabilized with pressure bandage and IV fluids, vitals steady.`
4. Restock medical inventory before returning to 10-8 status.

---

## 6. 🎒 Medical Inventory & Equipment Safety

* **Medkits & Bandages:** Used strictly for patient stabilization.
* **Blood Bags:** Require matching blood type RP or general O-Negative transfusion RP.
* **Defibrillators (AED):** Only use on non-responsive patients with cardiac arrest.
* **Restocking:** Restock your ambulance at designated hospital pharmacy points at the beginning and end of every shift.
